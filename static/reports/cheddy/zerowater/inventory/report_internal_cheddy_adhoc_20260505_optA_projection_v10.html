Mode for this report: Projection (Option A).
Projection view (Option A): Critical = projected stockout within 30 days even after every open PO lands on its expected date. Open POs included in the calculation.
out of stock
Available qty = 0 AND no FC transfer in motion. True stockout — no sellable inventory now and nothing returning to availability soon.
restocking
Available qty = 0 BUT FC Transfer reserved > 0. Operational transit — Amazon is moving units between fulfillment centers; they will return to Available within days. Not a real stockout.
critical
Projected to stock out within 30 days even after every open PO lands on its expected date.
warning
Projected to stock out within 31–60 days even after open POs land.
at risk
Projected to stock out within 61–90 days even after open POs land. Current PO pipeline is mathematically insufficient, but you have time.
excess
More than 180 days of cover at current velocity. Capital and storage fees tied up; consider promo, S&S targeting, or removal.
healthy
Covered through 90+ days with all open POs landing on time. Nothing to do today.
no sales
Replenishable but zero units sold in the last 30 days. Velocity unknown — review listing health and pricing.

Next Order Recommendation 4

What this answers. If you place a PO today as part of your regular 7-day reorder rhythm, here's the recommended quantity per SKU. Recommendations assume you'll place another PO again in 7 days — so each PO does the right amount of "this cycle's work," not a one-shot make-up that over-orders.

How the math works (per SKU).

  1. Build the planning baseline. Start from Sellable Equivalent (Available + FC Transfer + FC Processing) plus In Transit units (ordered, en route to Amazon). This is what we'll actually be able to ship in the near future.
  2. Project forward 31 days (the total lead time = 10d vendor + 21d outbound). Each day, subtract velocity (~30-day gross orders from order_report_data); each open PO arrival date adds its quantity. The result is "Projected stock at +31d" — what's left at the moment a brand-new PO would land.
  3. Compare to the SKU's target. Target = the per-SKU "Item Days of Stock" (or vendors.days_of_stock = 45 fallback) × velocity. The recommendation depends on the gap = target − projected:
CaseRecommendationWhy
Deficit
projected < target		order = gapCloses the deficit in this PO; next week's PO reverts to cycle base.
At target
projected = target		order = cycle base
= velocity × 7d		Maintains target at every subsequent PO arrival — steady-state replenishment.
Excess
projected > target		order = max(0, cycle base − excess)Reduces this week's order to absorb the surplus. If excess > one cycle, skip the week (order 0).

The "Case" hint under each recommendation tells you which branch the SKU fell into so you can sanity-check at a glance. Quantities round up to the nearest 10 — adjust for vendor minimums and case packs before submitting.

Trough column. The lowest projected cover (in days) at any point during the 90-day horizon. If it drops below 30 days, FBA assesses a low-inventory fee. Red trough values flag SKUs where the projection breaches the 30-day floor — note that a new PO landing at +31d may not fix a mid-cycle dip; expediting or bringing forward an existing PO is often the only operational lever.
